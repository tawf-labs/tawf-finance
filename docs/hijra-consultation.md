## A. The one-page offer (leave-behind)

**Headline:** *Grow your financing book without waiting for capital. We bring you
funding from outside your district, priced against your best serviced financing —
you keep origination and servicing, and you free up capacity to lend more.*

**The problem you feel every month:**
- Your deposit funding is capped near the LPS rate (6.25%) and can only be raised
  inside your operating area. It is expensive and it runs out.
- You have a financing pipeline you cannot fully serve because CAR/BMPK capacity
  is the binding constraint, not demand.
- Consolidation (POJK 7/2026) puts a clock on growth.

**What we do:**
- You select a pool of your *best, already-serviced* financing (clean SLIK record).
- Your DPS reviews and signs a sell-down akad (wakalah bil istithmar / musyarakah).
- An outside pool of KYC'd individual investors funds that pool in USDC.
- **You keep originating and servicing it** — you keep the customer relationship,
  the origination fee, and the servicing spread.
- The sold-down portion moves off your balance-sheet constraint, freeing CAR/BMPK
  headroom to originate the *next* pool.
- Funds sit in an audited escrow/vault, never with us; a licensed custodian holds
  custody. We are a technology vendor, not the bank, not the investment manager,
  not the Shariah authority.

**What you get:**
1. **Non-local funding** priced outside your deposit market.
2. **Released capacity** — the real prize: more room to grow the book.
3. **Retained economics** — origination + servicing revenue stays with you.
4. **A verifiable pool** — pool size, NPF, akad compliance provable continuously,
   *without exposing a single borrower* (zero-knowledge). Your book stays private.

**What we are NOT asking:**
- Not asking you to raise equity or sukuk (that needs IDR 80bn core capital).
- Not asking you to expose borrower identities or your financing book.
- Not asking for a big commitment — the pilot is one small pool (see Part C).

**One line:** *You originate and service; we bring the funding and the rails. You
grow the book you already can't fully fund, and you keep the relationship.*

## B. The worked economics (why the bank is net-better-off)

The whole deal turns on one inequality the bank must believe:

> **(origination fee + servicing spread retained + value of released capacity)
> > (financing yield given up on the sold-down pool)**

Walk through it live with Hijra's real numbers. Below is the structure with
illustrative placeholders.

### B.1 The naïve objection (what they'll think first)

*"I earn ~[18–22]% on this financing. Why would I hand it to investors at 12–15%?"*

This framing is wrong because it assumes the bank keeps the asset either way. It
can't — it is **capacity-constrained**. The real comparison is not "keep 20% vs
give up to 12%." It is **"earn nothing on financing I cannot fund vs earn a
servicing spread + free capacity on financing I now can."**

### B.2 The correct comparison (per IDR 1bn of pool, illustrative)

| Line | Without Tawf | With Tawf sell-down |
|---|---|---|
| Can the bank fund this pool? | **No** — capacity-constrained | Yes — funded by investor pool |
| Gross financing yield (~20%) | IDR 0 (not originated) | Given up to investors |
| Investor return (~12–15%) | — | Paid from the financing yield |
| **Origination fee retained** | IDR 0 | ~[x]% one-time |
| **Servicing spread retained** | IDR 0 | ~[3–6]% p.a. of pool |
| Tawf structuring/platform fee | — | ~[1–2.5]% (cost of the rail) |
| CAR/BMPK capacity released | None | Frees headroom to originate the next pool |
| **Net to bank** | **~IDR 0** on this pool | **Servicing spread + fees + capacity to grow** |

The number that matters is the **last row**. The counterfactual for a
capacity-constrained bank is *not originating the pool at all* — so any retained
servicing spread + released capacity is upside, not sacrifice.

### B.3 The capacity multiplier (the real prize)

Releasing capacity is not a one-pool benefit — it compounds:

- Suppose freeing capacity on IDR 1bn lets the bank originate another IDR 1bn.
- On that *new* pool the bank earns its full spread (or sells it down again and
  earns servicing + fees again).
- The sell-down converts a *stock* constraint (fixed balance sheet) into a *flow*
  business (originate → sell down → originate again). **That is the pitch: turn
  your balance sheet from a ceiling into a conveyor belt.**

Ask for, and fill in, Hijra's real figures:
1. Their current gross financing yield on the target segment. → `[__]%`
2. Their marginal cost of deposit funding today. → `[__]%`
3. Their current CAR and BMPK headroom (how constrained are they really?). → `[__]`
4. The servicing spread they'd need to retain to make it worthwhile. → `[__]%`
5. The max investor return they can support and still be net-positive. → `[__]%`

If (4)+(5)+capacity value clears their hurdle, the assumption holds and you have a
pilot. If it doesn't, you've learned the real reason — which is the most valuable
outcome of the trip either way.

## C. The pilot scope (small enough to say yes to)

Make the "yes" cheap. The pilot is designed to be low-risk, reversible, and fast.

**Pilot shape:**
- **One small pool:** IDR [500m–2bn] of the bank's *best* already-serviced
  financing in one segment (micro-trade or SME working capital — short tenor,
  clean SLIK).
- **Short tenor:** 30–60 day cycle (matches the live product parameters), so the
  full loop — fund → service → remit → redeem — completes inside ~2 months.
- **DPS-first:** nothing funds until Hijra's DPS reviews and signs the sell-down
  akad. The DPS signature *is* the gate. (This protects the bank and is your moat.)
- **Custody + escrow:** funds in an audited vault + licensed custodian; never with
  Tawf; never commingled.
- **Sandbox-aware:** structured to fit the OJK sandbox path (already tested
  securities tokenization); Tawf maps the IT-vendor obligations.

**What Tawf brings:**
- The registry, sell-down structuring, tokenization, settlement, verification UI.
- A cohort of KYC'd individual investors (allowlisted first) ready to fund.
- The regulatory mapping and the custodian/Sekuritas relationships.

**What Hijra brings:**
- One pool of serviced financing + the SLIK/quality data.
- DPS engagement to review the akad.
- Servicing (which they already do).

**Success = one real pool funded, one servicer remittance cycle, one redemption.**
That single loop converts the whole thesis from hypothesis to proof.

**Explicit off-ramps (why it's safe to try):**
- Pilot pool is tiny; downside is bounded.
- Bank keeps servicing and the customer relationship throughout.
- No equity, no sukuk, no borrower exposure, no lock-in.
- If it doesn't work, the bank has lost a small pool's structuring effort, nothing
  structural.

## D. Diagnostic questions — what you MUST learn in the room

These are ordered to test the central assumption first. Listen more than you pitch.

**On the binding constraint (the whole ballgame):**
1. Is balance-sheet capacity (CAR/BMPK) actually your growth constraint today — or
   is it deposit funding, pipeline, or something else? *(If capacity isn't the
   constraint, the whole thesis needs rethinking — this is the first thing to
   learn.)*
2. How much financing do you turn away or delay in a typical month for lack of
   capacity/funding? What is that costing you?
3. Where does your marginal funding come from now, and what does it cost?

**On willingness to sell down (the assumption itself):**
4. If an outside pool funded a segment of your book, what servicing spread would
   you need to retain for it to be worth doing?
5. What's the maximum all-in cost of funding (investor return + our fee) you could
   accept and still come out ahead?
6. Would you rather keep a smaller book at full spread, or grow a larger book at a
   servicing spread? *(This directly reveals the mindset the pivot bets on.)*

**On Shariah (the moat + the gate):**
7. Would your DPS engage on a wakalah bil istithmar / musyarakah sell-down akad?
   What would they need to sign it?
8. Any structures your DPS would reject outright? *(Learn the red lines early.)*

**On operations & regulation:**
9. Could you produce a clean, serviced pool (with SLIK data) for a pilot in the
   next quarter? Which segment is cleanest?
10. Any concern being an early participant in the OJK sandbox route with a tech
    vendor? What IT-vendor / procurement steps would we need to clear?

**On the relationship:**
11. What would make this a *yes* for you? What's the single biggest blocker?

*(Q11 is the most important question in the room — ask it explicitly.)*

## E. Objection handling

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| **"Why give up yield?"** | You're not giving up yield on financing you keep — you're earning a servicing spread + fees + capacity on financing you *currently can't fund at all*. The counterfactual is IDR 0, not full spread. |
| **"Crypto is risky / not Shariah."** | The investment asset is your financing, not crypto. USDC is a settlement rail, not the asset. The akad is DSN-MUI-referenced and *your DPS* signs it — we encode guidance, we don't author it. Funds sit in audited escrow + licensed custody, never with us. |
| **"We'd expose our book / borrowers."** | Never. Pool size, NPF, and akad compliance are provable with zero-knowledge — investors verify the pool is real without seeing a single borrower or contract. Your book stays private. |
| **"Is this even legal?"** | Exposure to bank financing is not a crypto asset; it's a *backed tokenized asset*. OJK's sandbox has already tested securities tokenization; we enter via the sandbox and map IT-vendor obligations with counsel. We're a vendor to you, the licensed bank — you stay inside your licence. |
| **"We can't commit resources."** | The pilot is one small, short-tenor pool of financing you already service. We bring the funding, rails, investors, and regulatory mapping. Your lift is one pool + DPS review. |
| **"What if it defaults?"** | Same NPF risk you already manage — you pick the pool. Default handling is explicit on-chain (principal-claim path); investors take defined risk, disclosed upfront. This is real serviced financing, not a guarantee. |
| **"Why you / why now?"** | Regulation just opened the backed-tokenized-asset path; onshore RWA is national policy; the KYC'd web3 investor base is 22.9m accounts and growing. First BPRS to run a compliant sell-down sets the template — and gets the funding channel first. |

## F. What "yes" looks like — leave with one of these

Ranked by strength (aim as high as they'll go):
1. **Signed pilot LOI** for one pool, DPS-first.
2. **DPS introduction** + agreement to review a draft akad.
3. **Their real numbers** on B.4 (yield, funding cost, capacity, hurdle spread) +
   a follow-up date.
4. **A clear no + the real reason** — this falsifies or reshapes the thesis and is
   worth the trip.

Do not leave with only "interesting, keep us posted." Push for a concrete next
step and a date.
